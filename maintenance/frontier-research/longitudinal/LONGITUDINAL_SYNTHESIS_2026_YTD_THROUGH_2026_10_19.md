## 3. Annual-to-date longitudinal argument
The useful 2026 observation under this repository's lens is not simply that new version numbers appeared. **from explicit handoff and approval authority to sandboxed, replayable, version-sensitive multi-tool trajectories** is further separated into versioned sources, processing and policy boundaries. Quarter I documents the first source-family distinctions in the new year; quarter J sharpens version/release behavior and the gap between a release claim and installed validation; quarter K exposes migration/compatibility or changed execution semantics. L contributes only dated October candidates, without a completed quarter.

Source influence is not assumed. Changes in selected release notes are not a field-wide controlled performance trend or proof of uniform ecosystem adoption. Any same-origin release family is counted as one publisher family despite multiple tags.

## 4. Hard boundaries, counter-reading, negative space
- claim indexed != claim true; score != probability; runtime-policy pass != truth; verification != scientific adjudication; provenance != truth.
- All upstream projects supply **primary release-description evidence**, not independent executions or scientifically validated downstream behavior.
- Strongest competing interpretation: a convenience sample of engineering release notes makes the year appear centered on our chosen lens; research-paper landscape, other vendors, negative deployments and reproducibility studies were not systematically included.
- Stage and month sources are version/date anchored, but local runtime, published experiment, independent appraisal and actor-specific adoption remain unverified.
- No 2026-Q4 quarter closure, Nov/Dec work, full annual review or 2026 end-of-year result is asserted.
