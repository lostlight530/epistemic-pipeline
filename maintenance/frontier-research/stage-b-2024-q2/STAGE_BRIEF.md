## Rationale

Stage A established the evidence envelope as a versioned object and showed that benchmark score, preference, proxy measurement, provider report, and support relation cannot collapse into one truth state

Stage B asks what happened when evaluation objects themselves became visibly stressed by long-context claims, benchmark corrections, contamination, saturation, prompt sensitivity, and time-dependent refresh

## Research questions

1. How should nominal capacity be distinguished from effective capability under harder condition sets
2. How do benchmark corrections and contamination change the evidence state of earlier observations
3. What comparison semantics are required when benchmarks become harder, less prompt-sensitive, or deliberately refreshed over time

## Hard boundaries

- benchmark score != universal capability
- claimed context length != effective context performance
- contamination signal != proven training-data lineage
- corrected benchmark != earlier observation never existed
- harder benchmark != scientific truth
- live benchmark != permanently contamination-free
- objective answer != complete construct validity
